There are three broad tiers of gear, and which one makes sense for you depends on budget, how serious you are about DJing, and whether you want a setup that mirrors what you'll find in actual clubs.

| Tier | What it is | Typical price range |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Controllers** | Small hardware unit \+ laptop running DJ software | \~£280 \- £1,400 |
| **All-in-one systems** | Standalone unit, no laptop required, club-inspired layout | \~£1,000 \- £3,150 |
| **Pro club gear** | Separate players \+ mixer, same as real venues | \~£6,800 \- £8,000+ for a full setup |

---

## **1\. Controllers**

These are the most affordable way in, and the best choice if you're not yet sure how serious you'll get. They connect to a laptop via USB and rely on software (rekordbox or Serato) to do the actual track playback and mixing.

**DDJ-FLX4**: \~£299 A compact 2-channel unit with a simple, uncluttered layout. The successor to the DDJ-400. Great if you just want to learn the basics without being overwhelmed by buttons. Downside: only 2 channels and a smaller feature set, so you'll likely outgrow it if you get serious.

**DDJ-FLX6**: \~£649 A 4-channel step up with a club-style layout that closely mirrors a real CDJ/mixer setup, so skills transfer well later. Comes with genuinely useful beginner-friendly features (Merge FX, Jog Cutter) as well as headroom to grow into. A great "buy once" option if you're fairly confident you'll stick with DJing.

**DDJ-1000**: \~£1,050-1,100 An older but still-respected 4-channel unit built specifically to feel like club gear (full-size jog wheels, proper mixer layout). More expensive and arguably being overtaken by the FLX6/FLX10, but still solid if found at a discount.

**DDJ-FLX10**: \~£1,350-1,400 The flagship controller: 4 channels, large jog wheels, stem/track-separation tech for on-the-fly mashups, and club-standard feel. This is "semi-pro" money for a laptop-based device. This one is sensible only if you want the absolute best controller experience and don't mind still needing a laptop.

**Overall:** controllers are the right call if you're budget-conscious, want to learn on your own schedule, or aren't sure DJing will stick. The tradeoff is you always need a laptop plugged in, which isn't how most venues actually work.

---

## **2\. All-in-one systems**

These sit between controllers and full pro gear. They run everything on the device itself (from USB drives, streaming, or cloud sync), while borrowing the touchscreen/jog wheel feel of real club equipment. Good for someone who's outgrown a controller and wants club-realistic practice, without paying full CDJ+mixer prices.

**XDJ-RX3**: \~£2,150-2,300 A 2-channel standalone system with a large 10.1" touchscreen and effects borrowed from club-standard gear. Very popular for home setups, mobile DJs, and livestreaming, since you don't need a laptop at all. Downside: only 2 channels, and it lacks the newer AZ's streaming/Wi-Fi extras.

**XDJ-XZ**: \~£2,600+ (older model, being phased out) A previous-generation 4-channel standalone unit. Still capable and sometimes available at a discount, but technologically behind the newer AZ (smaller screen, no streaming, no Bluetooth). Worth considering only if you find a good used/clearance price.

**XDJ-AZ**: \~£3,150 *(new flagship, replaces the XDJ-XZ)* A genuine 4-channel standalone system that borrows heavily from the CDJ-3000/DJM-A9 pro gear, with a big touchscreen, full-size jog wheels, streaming, Wi-Fi, wireless headphone support. This is about as close as you can get to a "real club setup" without buying separate players and a mixer, and it's priced accordingly.

**XDJ-AN**: \~£999-1,050 *(new model, released 2026\)* A compact, lightweight 2-channel standalone unit built specifically as a stepping-stone between a home controller and a club booth. Its layout is modelled on the CDJ/DJM workflow. Streams from Apple Music/TIDAL/Beatport and syncs with rekordbox cloud libraries. Smaller screen and jog wheels than the pricier units below, and Wi-Fi only (no wired network features). It's the cheapest genuinely "club-mapped" standalone option, but the reason they were able to make it cheaper is that a lot of the features found as physical buttons or knobs on older systems are moved to the touchscreen on this model, so if you learn on this, you'll have less transferrable "muscle memory" to the other older units where these features are physical controls, and vice-versa.

**Overall:** all-in-ones are a strong pick for someone with a real budget who wants the club-standard feel and standalone convenience (no laptop, easy for mobile/wedding-style DJs), without the expense and extra footprint of separate players and a mixer.

## **3\. Pro club gear**

This is what you'll find in most professional clubs and festivals: individual media players (CDJs) connected to a separate standalone mixer. It's the most expensive and least "plug and play" option, but it's exactly what you'll be using if you ever DJ at an actual venue, so there's no adjustment period.

**CDJ-3000**: \~£2,000-2,100 each (you need at least two) The long-running club-standard player, found in the vast majority of professional booths worldwide. Excellent build quality and a huge installed base, meaning huge familiarity value, but it's gradually being replaced by the newer 3000X.

**CDJ-3000X**: \~£2,350-2,450 each *(newer model)* The next-generation flagship player: adds Wi-Fi, cloud library sync, streaming service support, NFC pairing with your phone, and a larger touchscreen on top of everything the 3000 already did well. The clear choice if buying new, though the price premium over the 3000 is meaningful.

**DJM-A9 mixer**: \~£2,100-2,300 The current flagship club mixer, needed alongside your CDJs since none of these players include a mixer built in. Professional-grade EQ, effects, and connectivity. Older mixers like the DJM-900NXS2 are cheaper and still widely used if you don't need the latest features.

**A typical starter "pro" setup**: two CDJ-3000X units plus a DJM-A9 mixer bundles for around **£6,800-£7,200**, though buying older CDJs with an older mixer can bring the total down meaningfully (used/older units often trade for noticeably less).

**Overall:** this tier only makes sense once you're confident you want to perform in real venues and want zero relearning when you get behind a club setup. It's a significant investment for a hobbyist, and honestly not necessary just for practicing at home, an all-in-one system gets you 90% of the "same feel" for a third of the price.

---

## **Quick recommendation summary**

* **Just starting out, modest budget:** DDJ-FLX4  
* **Fairly confident you'll stick with it, want room to grow:** DDJ-FLX6  
* **Want the best controller money can buy (but still need a laptop):** DDJ-FLX10  
* **Want standalone convenience without laptop, moderate budget:** XDJ-AN or XDJ-RX3  
* **Want the closest thing to "real club gear" in one box:** XDJ-AZ  
* **Serious about performing in actual venues, budget isn't the main constraint:** CDJ-3000X pair \+ DJM-A9
